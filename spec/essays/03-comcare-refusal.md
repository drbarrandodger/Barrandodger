# 03 — Comcare’s 26 May 2021 refusal of claim 1326583/1

## Executive proposition

On 26 May 2021 Comcare delegate Amy Delzoppo declined a workers’ compensation claim for an anxiety state and depressive disorder. The stated reason was that Comcare was not satisfied the claimant was an “employee” under section 5 of the Safety, Rehabilitation and Compensation Act 1988 (Cth), so the claim was precluded. The injury date on the letter is 22 January 2021. The claim number is 1326583/1.

## Scope

The letter is the primary determination. The August 2021 affirmation is described in the later Tribunal reasons and was not opened as a separate PDF in this pass.

## Documentary findings

The letter is addressed to Dr Richard William McLean at 2 McCubbin Street, Footscray. It says the claim form of 26 April 2021 described the usual occupation as artist, life coach and therapeutic supplier, and the claimant as a sole trader. An email of 13 May 2021 is recorded as saying he was a registered NDIA provider, paid through PRODA, and an employer of himself.

The letter sets out ordinary indicators of a contract of service: power to hire, wages with tax and superannuation, supervision and control, and subjection to the APS Code of Conduct.

It records that the NDIA, in a statement dated 10 May 2021, said he was not an NDIA employee, was not paid wages with tax and superannuation deducted, was not supervised or controlled in how he worked, and was not subject to the APS Code or internal NDIA policies.

It records his reply of 14 May 2021: that he contracted to the NDIA, that the NDIA could end the relationship, that he was paid by the NDIA, and that NDIS Commission audit and reportable-incident rules applied. Comcare acknowledges he was a registered provider, had to meet practice standards, and was paid for supports. It says a copy of his contract was requested and had not been received. It concludes the material supports an independent sole trader under a contract for services, not a contract of service.

Review rights are stated: reconsideration within 30 days.

## Corroboration

The 12 July 2023 Tribunal reasons describe this determination and the August 2021 affirmation, and affirm the reviewable decision. That is a later official determination of the same claim pathway, not a duplicate photocopy.

The NDIA denial of 10 May 2021 is recited in both the Comcare letter and the Tribunal reasons. The NDIA statement itself was not opened as its own file in this pass. It is corroborated as a recited official response, not re-read from the NDIA’s own letterhead.

## Contrary evidence

The claimant’s emails, as recited, argue the opposite: payment by the NDIA and regulatory control meant employment. Comcare records those points and does not accept them. The Federal Court General Counsel letter in 2023 uses PID Act language of employment with DSS. That letter is later, uses a different Act, and is addressed in essays 01 and 02. It does not rewrite this determination.

## What the record establishes

- A — Comcare declined claim 1326583/1 on 26 May 2021 for anxiety state and depressive disorder on the employee criterion.
- A — The letter records the claim form’s sole-trader description and the NDIA’s denial of employment.

## What the record does not establish

It does not establish that no psychological injury occurred, that the NDIA never paid him, or that the delegate acted for an improper purpose. Motive is not in the letter.

## Unresolved questions

The contract Comcare asked for is not in this letter. The August 2021 reconsideration letter was not opened separately.

## Evidence classification

Determination: A, official record.

Claimant’s characterisation of himself as an employee: C, recorded in the letter as his position, rejected by the delegate.
