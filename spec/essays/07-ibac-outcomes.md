# 07 — Two IBAC decisions, and two different sentences about protection

## Executive proposition

IBAC wrote twice. On 9 April 2020 it decided a complaint about Victoria Police and AHPRA was not a public-interest complaint and would not be investigated or referred. On 30 October 2023 it decided complaints about Victoria Police, Western Health and other bodies would not be investigated or referred, and that the complaint was not a public-interest disclosure. The 2020 letter says Part 6 protections of the Victorian PID Act still apply. The 2023 letter says they do not. Those are different outcomes. They are not one continuous finding of corruption, and they are not one continuous finding of innocence.

## Scope

Two short IBAC letters. Both were text-extracted. A third assessment PDF dated 12 April 2022 did not yield readable text in this pass and is not used.

## Documentary findings

9 April 2020, CASE-2020712, to Mr Richard Mclean at rich@richmclean.com.au. Complaint received 4 March 2020 against Victoria Police and AHPRA. IBAC assessed it under the Public Interest Disclosures Act 2012 (Vic). It determined the complaint should not be treated as a public-interest complaint. It decided not to investigate or refer, under section 67 of the IBAC Act. It says the information does not show or tend to show that the public officer engaged in improper conduct or detrimental action. It also says that although it is not a public-interest complaint, protections under Part 6 of the PID Act still apply. A sentence begins “AHPRA is not a public body” and was cut off in the extract; this essay does not finish that sentence.

30 October 2023, CASE-20231101 and CASE-20233846, document CD/23/132002, to Richard William McLean. Complaints received 20 February and 13 June 2023 about Victoria Police, Western Health and other unspecified agencies. IBAC apologises for the time taken and acknowledges distress. It decided not to investigate or refer because there was not enough information to show police misconduct or corrupt conduct, again citing section 67. It says the investigation threshold is high and includes serious or systemic corrupt conduct. It determines the complaint is not a public-interest disclosure, “which means that you will not receive protections under the PID Act.”

## Corroboration

These are two decisions of the same agency about different complaints. The second does not independently corroborate the first. No external agency’s investigation of the same IBAC complaints was opened in this scan.

## Contrary evidence

The letters are adverse to a claim that IBAC investigated and found corrupt conduct. They are also adverse to a claim that IBAC found the underlying events did not happen. Both letters are threshold decisions: not a public-interest complaint, and not enough to investigate. The 2020 letter still attaches Part 6 protections; the 2023 letter does not. Any archive sentence that merges “IBAC rejected me” into a single legal consequence is less precise than the letters.

The 2020 letter says the information does not show improper conduct. That is IBAC’s assessment of the information it had, under its statute. It is not a criminal acquittal of any officer.

## What the record establishes

- A — Two written IBAC decisions not to investigate or refer, on the dates and case numbers above.
- A — The protection sentence in 2020 and the protection sentence in 2023 are different.

## What the record does not establish

It does not establish that Victoria Police or Western Health acted lawfully in any particular incident. It does not establish the content of the complaints beyond the agencies named. It does not establish that AHPRA was or was not a public body beyond the incomplete sentence.

## Unresolved questions

The 12 April 2022 assessment file did not yield text. The rest of the 2020 AHPRA sentence was not recovered from the extract.

## Evidence classification

Both decisions: A, official records, threshold decisions.

Allegations inside the complaints: C. Their truth was not determined by these letters.

## Conclusion

IBAC declined to investigate these complaints. In 2020 it still said Part 6 protections applied. In 2023 it said the complaint was not a public-interest disclosure and those protections did not apply. That difference is part of the record. Neither letter is a finding of a criminal conspiracy, and neither letter is a finding that every complaint was untrue.
