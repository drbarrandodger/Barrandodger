# 06 — APRA’s 5 November 2021 refusal to treat the report as a PID

## Executive proposition

In a letter dated 5 November 2021, APRA told Dr Rich McLean that the matters in his correspondence were not within APRA’s remit and would not be treated as a public interest disclosure under the PID Act or as a whistleblower complaint under the Corporations Act 2001.

## Scope

The letter is embedded in a later email chain saved as a PDF. The chain includes his reply. His reply is not APRA’s decision.

## Documentary findings

The APRA text is dated 5 November 2021, addressed by email to richarddrawsstuff@gmail.com, headed as a report of misconduct to APRA. It refers to emails of 27 May 2021 and 21 October 2021 to PID and whistleblower addresses, and to later correspondence. It notes that he referred APRA to www.richmclean.com.au and forwarded correspondence with other organisations.

APRA describes itself as the supervisor of banking, insurance and superannuation. It then states that, having considered the issues, it does not consider the matters within its remit and will not treat the matter as a PID or as a Corporations Act whistleblower complaint. It will not keep responding unless further information relevant to its remit is provided.

The same passage says APRA is concerned about the nature of the latest correspondence with Peter Dunstan. The extract available to this synthesis cuts off before that concern is fully stated. This essay does not invent the missing sentences.

A separate thread in another PDF shows the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner, reference EN21/12857, writing on 3 November 2021 that it does not act on letters merely courtesy-copied to it. That is an OAIC filing practice, not a finding about APRA.

## Corroboration

No second prudential agency was found in this scan confirming APRA’s remit decision. The Ombudsman and Federal Court decisions are different statutes and different facts. They are not corroboration that the conduct was outside APRA’s remit. They are separate non-investigations.

## Contrary evidence

His covering email says Peter Dunstan rejected whistleblower status because Dunstan rejected that he was a government employee, and that other evidence counteracts that. The APRA text extracted here does not give “not a government employee” as the reason. It gives remit. If another APRA sentence says something else, it was not in the extract. The author’s summary and the letter’s stated reason should not be collapsed into one sentence.

## What the record establishes

- A — APRA stated on 5 November 2021 that it would not treat the matter as a PID or Corporations Act whistleblower complaint, because it considered the matters outside its remit.
- A — OAIC stated it does not open enquiries that are only courtesy copied.

## What the record does not establish

It does not establish that no misconduct occurred in a bank, insurer, or superannuation fund. It establishes that this correspondence was not taken up by APRA as a PID. It does not establish the content of the concern about correspondence with Peter Dunstan beyond the fact that a concern is announced.

## Unresolved questions

The remainder of the Dunstan concern, and any later APRA letter, were not extracted. The website he sent APRA was not re-crawled as at 2021.

## Evidence classification

APRA outcome: A, official record, limited to remit and non-treatment as a PID.

Author’s explanation of Dunstan’s reason: C, his characterisation, not adopted as APRA’s stated reason.

APRA refused this report as a disclosure within its scheme and said why: remit. That refusal is not a finding that every institution named elsewhere in the archive acted lawfully, and it is not a finding that they conspired.
