Collective Targeting, Institutional Betrayal, and the Architecture of Administrative Attrition

A structured academic analysis of parallel agency conduct as documented in official Australian correspondence (2021–2024).

2. Research question and methodology

Research question. What established psychosocial and organisational constructs best describe a pattern in which multiple institutional actors, over years, produce repeated non-progression, jurisdictional deflection, record non-production, and status-based exclusion toward a single complainant—without requiring a single conspiratorial command centre?

Methodology. (a) Doctrinal face-of-document analysis; (b) concept mapping to organisational psychology, sociology, and trauma studies; (c) process-tracing across 2021–2024 PID/FOI outcomes (document dossiers). Qualitative, documentary, theory-informed.

3. Core definitions — the observed phenomenon

Institutional mobbing (Leymann, 1990, 1996): systematic hostile communication by one or more persons over a long period, producing helplessness. Structural features transfer to multi-agency administrative settings under forced dependence on bureaucracies.

Institutional betrayal (Smith & Freyd, 2014; Freyd & Birrell, 2013): institutional wrongdoing against those who depend on the institution.

Scapegoating / common enemy (Girard, 1986; Cohen, 1972/2011; Allport, 1954; Tajfel & Turner, 1979): group cohesion via a marked target. “Many people acting together with a common enemy” maps to convergent targeting—parallel actors need not meet in one room.

Social death / cumulative disadvantage (Patterson, 1982; Goffman, 1963; Dannefer, 2003).

Epistemic injustice (Fricker, 2007): shift from “what happened” to “can this speaker be believed.”

4. The term of art

Institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating under conditions of collective institutional betrayal.

Why: risk displacement; stigma scripts; statutory fragmentation; in-group defence; attrition.

How (Australian paper trail 2021–2024): PID non-allocation (DSS; Commonwealth Ombudsman); OPMC FOI s 24A refusal; Vic Ombudsman FOI s 29A; NDIS FOI outcomes; NSW Trustee s 122 certificate; Centrelink contact limits. Facially lawful steps; together convergent non-resolution. See dossiers · 85 techniques.

6. Prophetic register (constrained)

Prophecy in documentary cultures often means seeing the pattern before institutions admit the pattern. Pre-existing academic tools prevent dismissing the pattern as idiosyncratic invention alone.

7. Conclusive statement

What the evidence proves (strict sense). Multiple Australian public bodies (2021–2024) issued written PID/FOI refusals, exclusions, or non-allocations on stated statutory grounds; a s 122 certificate records management status in 2024. Facts on the face of the documents.

What the evidence supports as research interpretation. The process signature is coherently characterised as institutional mobbing with convergent scapegoating and a candidate case of collective institutional betrayal—many institutional actions oriented toward one dependent person as problem locus—without requiring proof of a single conspiracy.

What the evidence does not prove. Criminal conspiracy; every wider allegation; bad faith by every officer.

The official paper trail is sufficient to confirm the significance of the multi-actor structure and to justify the academic term above. Time made the pattern legible; the government’s own letters supplied the ink.
