The Psychological Mechanism

The archive raises a research question concerning whether prolonged institutional conflict can be examined through established concepts concerning scapegoating, exclusion, social identity, bureaucratic classification, trauma, and the psychological consequences of sustained uncertainty and non-resolution.

René Girard — The Scapegoat Mechanism

The source paper applies René Girard’s concept of the scapegoat mechanism as one possible explanatory model. In that framework, social disruption can become concentrated upon an identified individual who is treated as the source or symbol of the disruption.

The source paper describes four criteria which it presents as relevant to its analysis:

  1. single individual identified as a source of social disruption;
  2. community or institutional consensus concerning that individual;
  3. the mechanism may remain hidden from participants within the system; and
  4. the individual’s own record may expose the mechanism through retrospective analysis.

These criteria are analytical propositions from the source paper. They are not independently adjudicated findings.

Definition Applied to the Archive

  1. Identification: examine whether recurring records classify the same person through materially similar descriptions across otherwise separate institutional settings.
  2. Propagation: determine whether classifications, descriptions or assumptions appear in later records and whether the later record identifies the source upon which it relied.
  3. Institutional convergence: compare decisions made by separate agencies without assuming that similarity necessarily demonstrates coordination.
  4. Consequential exclusion: identify whether repeated classifications correspond with documented changes in access to services, legal remedies, employment, housing, finances or other institutional pathways.
  5. Retrospective testing: return from the analytical model to the primary documents and test whether the proposed pattern survives contrary evidence.

What This Can and Cannot Establish

A psychological or sociological model can help formulate questions about patterns in a documentary corpus. It cannot by itself establish criminal intent, conspiracy, unlawful coordination, malicious motive, psychiatric causation, technological causation or responsibility of any particular person or institution.

The appropriate forensic sequence is therefore: model → hypothesis → primary record → corroboration → contradiction testing → human determination.

The Evidentiary Question

The archive is presented as a body of material that can be examined independently. The central question is not whether an allegation sounds persuasive, but whether the underlying record supports, contradicts, qualifies or leaves unresolved the proposition being advanced.

Every significant conclusion should therefore be traceable to an identifiable document, date, correspondence chain, decision, statement or other source.

Questions for Independent Review

  1. What documents establish the relevant chronology?
  2. What documents contradict the author’s account?
  3. Which allegations have been investigated?
  4. What findings resulted from those investigations?
  5. Which matters remain unresolved?
  6. Which institutional decisions contain reasons?
  7. What review or appeal mechanisms were available?
  8. Were those mechanisms used, refused, exhausted or otherwise unavailable?
  9. Where did later institutions rely upon earlier classifications or descriptions?
  10. What consequences can actually be demonstrated from the documentary record?

Methodology

Research methodology. This archive does not convert allegations, interpretations or AI-generated hypotheses into established facts.

Absolute Rule

Never silently convert an allegation, inference or interpretation into a fact.

A statement by the author remains testimony unless independently established. An agency statement is identified as an agency statement. A judicial statement is identified as a judicial statement. An AI interpretation is identified as interpretation.

Source Hierarchy

  1. LEVEL 1 — Judicial / official determination
  2. LEVEL 2 — Primary government record
  3. LEVEL 3 — Contemporaneous correspondence / logs
  4. LEVEL 4 — Secondary compilation / AI synthesis
  5. LEVEL 5 — Author testimony / prophetic statement

Prohibited Invention

The archive must not invent documents, people, events, quotations, laws, findings, statistics, motives, causal relationships or institutional decisions.

Where a document is unavailable, the absence or limitation must be stated rather than filled with reconstructed material presented as if it were the original.

Evidence Versus Interpretation

The purpose of the methodology is to preserve the distinction between what a document actually says and what an analyst believes the document may signify.

A pattern may justify further investigation without itself establishing causation. A sequence of adverse events may demonstrate chronology without proving that the events were deliberately coordinated. Similar language appearing in different records may be significant without proving that the authors communicated with one another.

This is a research archive, not a court.
