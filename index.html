05 · Forensic Evidence Centre

Evidence Architecture

Primary Evidence

Original documents and source records.

L1–L3 preferred

Government Record

Official correspondence, FOI releases, proceedings and administrative records.

L1–L2

Independent Corroboration

Evidence from independent sources capable of supporting or challenging an assertion.

L4

Author Record

Statements, testimony and contemporaneous accounts produced by the author.

L5

Secondary Material

News, commentary, reviews and other secondary material.

L6

Interpretation

Analytical or theoretical interpretation of documentary material.

L7

Unverified Claim

Claims for which the underlying evidence has not yet been independently established.

L8
Evidence rule: A filename is not a finding. A hyperlink is not corroboration. A repository entry is not a judicial determination. A cryptographic timestamp can demonstrate that a digital object existed in a particular form at a particular time; it does not by itself prove the truth of statements contained in that object.

09 · Media & Video

Audiovisual Record

Video material is maintained separately from documentary evidence. A video may be useful corroborating, contextual, testimonial or interpretive material, but the existence of a video does not automatically establish the truth of its claims.

Video Forensics Protocol

  1. Record the original public URL.
  2. Record publication date where available.
  3. Record channel / author.
  4. Preserve the title exactly as published.
  5. Preserve transcript or captions where legally/publicly available.
  6. Identify claims made by the video.
  7. Map each claim to documentary sources separately.
  8. Record corroboration, contradiction or unresolved status.

Do not convert an AI interpretation of a video into a documentary fact. The video remains the primary audiovisual object; the analysis remains analysis.

10 · Verification

Independent Verification Protocol

1. Repository Existence

Confirm that the named path exists in the public repository.

2. File Type

Determine whether the object is a complete document or merely a pointer/stub.

3. Byte Size

Compare the actual file size with the relevant manifest where one exists.

4. SHA-256

Calculate SHA-256 against the actual bytes of the complete file.

5. Provenance

Record where the document originated and how it entered the archive.

6. External Comparison

Where possible, compare against the original issuing institution or independent repository.

7. Status Label

Use labels such as official record, primary document, author account, allegation, analysis or unverified claim.

8. Reproducibility

Give another researcher enough information to repeat the check without relying on the author's interpretation.

Current verification limitation: The public verification portal reports that some repository paths are complete git objects while others are pointer/stub files, and that its inspected sample did not establish a matching SHA-256 for the listed manifest entries. Therefore this master page does not describe the entire repository as cryptographically verified.

11 · Research Method

Methodology

1 · Preserve

Preserve the document in its original form together with filename, date, source and identifying information.

2 · Catalogue

Assign each record an archive category and unique catalogue identifier where practical.

3 · Cross-reference

Connect related people, organisations, dates, documents, responses, events and legal instruments.

4 · Verify

Where possible, independently inspect the file, calculate hashes and identify original provenance.

5 · Separate

Never merge an allegation, author's statement or AI interpretation into the primary documentary record.

6 · Preserve Access

Maintain multiple public copies where legally and technically possible.

7 · State Uncertainty

If something cannot be independently established, label it as unresolved rather than silently upgrading it.

8 · Allow Falsification

Researchers should be able to locate contradictory evidence, corrections and alternative explanations.

15 · Preservation

Distributed Preservation Architecture

The archive uses a distributed-preservation model: multiple public websites, GitHub repositories, GitHub Pages deployments and independently accessible document locations can provide redundancy.

Repository

Preserve the source files in a public repository.

Published Mirror

Maintain a GitHub Pages or equivalent published representation.

Independent Mirror

Maintain another public node where technically and legally appropriate.

Hash

Calculate SHA-256 for complete source files when verification is required.

Manifest

Maintain a machine-readable manifest linking filename, source, date, size and hash.

Human-readable Index

Provide a researcher-friendly catalogue explaining what each document is.

16 · Legal / Evidentiary Notice

How This Archive Should Be Read

This is a public research and archival presentation. It is not a court, government investigation, legal opinion or determination of liability.

The archive may contain allegations, author testimony, correspondence, official documents, legal arguments, interpretations, AI-generated analyses, historical material and secondary sources.

A document may establish that a statement was made or that an event was recorded without establishing that every assertion within that document is true.

Likewise, the presence of a person's name, an organisation's name, a legal term, a filename or an allegation does not establish criminality, misconduct, liability or causation.

Readers should inspect the underlying documents, distinguish source types, consider contrary evidence and form their own conclusions.

17 · Corrections & Research

Corrections, Sources & Further Research

If a catalogue entry is inaccurate, incomplete, duplicated or incorrectly classified, the preferred correction is to identify the exact record, the disputed field and the source supporting the correction.

The archive should preserve corrections rather than silently rewriting historical versions wherever practical.

Research standard: The objective is not to make the archive more persuasive. The objective is to make the archive more inspectable.
