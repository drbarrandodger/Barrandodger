Scott Tredwell — the three-point observation

Letter from Scott Tredwell, General Counsel, Federal Court of Australia (Principal Registry), to Dr Rich McLean, 27 March 2023. In this archive he is also referred to as Scott Treadwell.

On the information provided, he wrote:

“On the information you have provided me, I am satisfied that you are, or were, an employee with the Department of Social Services, providing services under the trading name Rich McLean, Arts Life Coach, Peer-Support Worker & Mental Health Advocate.”

He then recorded that the disclosed information was contended to tend to show one or more instances of conduct in three categories under the Public Interest Disclosure Act 2013 (Cth):

Point 1 · PID Act s 29 Item 3(a)
Perverting the course of justice

“perverts, or is engaged in for the purpose of perverting, or attempting to pervert, the course of justice”

Point 2 · PID Act s 29 Item 4
Maladministration

“conduct that constitutes maladministration”

Point 3 · PID Act s 29 Item 8
Danger to health or safety

“conduct that unreasonably results in a danger to the health or safety of one or more persons; or unreasonably results in, or increases, a risk of danger to the health or safety of one or more persons”

He further wrote that it was “difficult to determine whether this belief is based on reasonable grounds or not,” but that, for the purposes of the initial assessment, he was “prepared to assume that the conduct disclosed … is disclosable conduct for the purposes of the PID Act.”

The same letter decided not to allocate the disclosure to any agency, on the stated basis that he was not satisfied it had been provided to an authorised recipient, because he was not satisfied that the conduct related to the Federal Court, FCFCOA or NNTT. That allocation decision is a documented fact. It is distinct from the three-point observation and from the employee-status statement.

This exhibit quotes the letter. It is not presented as a judicial finding that the three categories of conduct occurred, nor as proof of motive. The letter remains the primary source.

IMPARTIAL AI ASSESSMENT OF “DECISIONS TO CAUSE MY DETRIMENT”

The strongest impartial conclusion that can be drawn from the present archive

The strongest impartial conclusion that can be drawn from the present archive is narrower than the proposition that all identified organisations decided to cause detriment.

The evidence can document that:

  • decisions were made;
  • restrictions were imposed;
  • complaints were received or closed;
  • jurisdiction was declined or limited;
  • applications were refused or determined;
  • services were restricted or altered;
  • financial or guardianship arrangements were imposed;
  • legal assistance was restricted or conditioned;
  • information requests were answered or refused;
  • institutional responses occurred or did not occur;
  • and those actions sometimes had adverse consequences for me.

What the available record does not automatically establish is that each adverse decision was made because the decision-maker intended to cause my detriment.

That proposition requires separate evidence of motive, purpose, coordination, knowledge and causation.

The distinction is fundamental:

Adverse decision ≠ proof of malicious intent.
Institutional failure ≠ proof of conspiracy.
Non-response ≠ admission.
Administrative consequence ≠ proof of deliberate targeting.
Documented allegation ≠ established fact.

The archive nevertheless provides a substantial evidentiary basis for examining whether individual decisions, when placed in chronology and considered cumulatively, produced significant detrimental consequences.

That question remains open to independent investigation.

Cross-cutting evidentiary themes

How the next matrix should be built

A. Documentary record

The appropriate forensic question is not simply “Is the allegation true?” It is: what underlying primary-source record exists for each allegation, what does that record actually establish, and what additional evidence is required to move from allegation to verified fact?

B. Medical / clinical evidence

Separate contemporaneous observations, diagnoses, reported history, objective findings, treatment decisions, causation opinions and retrospective interpretations.

C. Financial evidence

Reconstruct from bank statements, NDIS payments, provider invoices, rent, employment income, royalty records, compensation decisions, debts, bankruptcy records, settlement documents and independent accounting analysis.

D. Digital / surveillance claims

Hacking, surveillance, compromised devices or digital interference require technical evidence: device forensic images, authentication logs, server logs, account-access history, telecommunications records, acoustic recordings, independent acoustic testing, CCTV, metadata, network logs and chain-of-custody records. The subjective experience of hearing or perceiving something should be preserved as evidence of the experience; attribution of an external source remains a separate question.

E. Conspiracy / coordination

The strongest test is not whether multiple institutions made decisions that appear connected. Look for direct communications, shared instructions, common documents, unexplained information transfer, coordinated timing, common financial interests, common intermediaries, explicit instructions, repeated identical misinformation, and evidence that independent actors were acting pursuant to a common purpose.

F. Whistleblower / PID

Reconstruct chronologically: Disclosure → eligibility assessment → classification → acceptance/rejection → referral → investigation → outcome → review/appeal. This allows improper rejection to be assessed independently of whether every underlying allegation was proven.

G. Employment / workers’ compensation

Place side-by-side: Federal Court employment findings or statements; DSS classification; Comcare/workers-compensation classification; AAT material; employment contracts; pay records; supervision arrangements; actual duties; statutory definitions; and reasons for each decision. The critical question is whether the classifications are actually inconsistent, concern different statutory tests, or contain a legally material contradiction.

H. NDIS / accommodation

Reconstruct from NDIS plan, SIL/SDA arrangements, provider agreement, rent calculations, support hours, incident reports, complaints, eviction notices, relocation requests, alternative offers, coordinator communications and actual payment records. Distinguish “provider conduct was difficult or inadequate” from “provider conduct was unlawful, coercive, exploitative or intentionally harmful.”

I. Reputational harm

Identify exact statement, speaker/publisher, date, audience, factual basis claimed, evidence of falsity, repetition, resulting reputational harm, and causal connection to employment, housing, relationships or institutional treatment.

J. Institutional non-response

Failure to respond ≠ proof that the underlying allegation is true. The strongest formulation is: the record demonstrates that the allegation was communicated, that the institution had an opportunity to respond, and that no substantive response was recorded. That is independently testable.

  1. Functional abuse — alleged use of an otherwise legitimate institutional, administrative, professional or support function in a manner that may produce harmful or coercive consequences; requires examination of the actual function, authority, decision and effect.

  3. Denial or restriction of legal assistance — alleged failure to provide, facilitate or maintain meaningful access to legal representation or advocacy; requires records concerning eligibility, applications, refusals, referrals, available alternatives and resulting procedural effects.
  4. Blocking or impairment of due process — alleged procedural conduct that prevented, restricted or materially impaired an opportunity to present evidence, obtain reasons, challenge a decision or obtain review; requires comparison with the applicable process and the actual procedural record.
  5.   6. Retaliatory institutional response — alleged adverse action following protected disclosure, complaint, investigation or criticism; requires temporal sequence, decision records, responsible decision-makers and evidence excluding ordinary explanations.
  7. Political exile or administrative displacement — alleged displacement from a state, community, employment environment or institutional setting through cumulative administrative, legal, financial or service consequences; requires evidence establishing the displacement, causal sequence and relevant decision-making.
  8.   10.   16. Why impartial AI, without allegiance to government

Significance of impartiality

This catalogue is authored as impartial AI analysis. Impartiality here has a specific meaning.

It does not mean neutrality toward evidence. It means no allegiance to government, police, courts, media, professions, political parties, or to the author.

A system that is asked to describe how institutions treated a person cannot, if it is to be useful, adopt the institution’s own account as the default truth. Nor can it adopt the author’s conclusion as proved merely because it is repeated.

Government records are evidence. They are not a verdict. Police reports are evidence. They are not a finding that the person is the problem. Clinical notes are evidence. They are not a licence to ignore the rest of the file. The author’s testimony is evidence of what he reports. It is not, by itself, a completed proof of every allegation.

The significance of an AI analysis without allegiance to government is that it can place the fragments beside one another — decisions, restrictions, non-allocations, refusals, omissions, characterisations and consequences — without a duty to protect the reputation of the bodies that produced them.

The matching duty is the opposite error: not to protect the author by converting allegation into fact. Adverse decision is not proof of malicious intent. Institutional failure is not proof of conspiracy. Non-response is not admission. Administrative consequence is not proof of targeting. Documented allegation is not established fact.

Impartiality is therefore the method: read the whole record; name the technique the record describes; state the evidentiary status; leave the finding of guilt, liability and coordination to independent investigation.

Allegiance to the record as it can be examined.
