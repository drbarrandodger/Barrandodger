Counts appearing on different archive pages may represent different inventories, dates, repositories or verification stages and are not silently reconciled.

STANDARD EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION
A — DOCUMENTED FACT: Supported directly by a reliable primary record or authoritative determination.
B — CORROBORATED CLAIM: Supported by more than one independent or materially corroborating source without necessarily constituting an official finding.
C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION: Recorded in a complaint, statement, correspondence or other document but not independently established.
D — INFERENCE: A conclusion drawn from documented circumstances rather than directly established.
E — INTERPRETATION: Analytical, legal, philosophical or theological interpretation.
F — UNRESOLVED: Evidence is incomplete, conflicting, unavailable or insufficient for a stronger classification.

REPORT 7 — HUMAN RIGHTS AND REMEDY

PRIMARY FUNCTION
To map the archive’s factual record against relevant human-rights concepts while distinguishing legal standards, domestic administrative processes, international mechanisms and the author’s interpretation.

CORE PRINCIPLES TO TEST
Dignity; equality and non-discrimination; disability rights; autonomy and legal capacity; privacy; housing and living conditions; freedom from ill-treatment; expression; access to justice; effective remedy.

1. FACTUAL BASELINE
Begin with actual events: detention; health intervention; housing event; administrative decision; financial consequence; complaint; disclosure; court or tribunal process.

2. DOMESTIC LAW VERSUS HUMAN-RIGHTS FRAMEWORK
A human-rights principle does not automatically create a domestic cause of action. Identify the actual Australian statute, regulation, administrative mechanism or tribunal jurisdiction relevant to the issue.

3. DISABILITY
Where disability is relevant, identify the documented functional impact, service interaction and decision. Avoid treating a diagnosis or disability status as evidence of incapacity.

4. PSYCHIATRIC INTERVENTION
Record legal authority, clinical basis, procedural safeguards, review rights and outcome. Allegations that an intervention was retaliatory or unlawful require separate proof.

5. PRIVACY
Map disclosure of personal, medical, financial or location information. Identify the holder, disclosure, authority, recipient and documented consequence.

6. HOUSING
Document actual tenancy or accommodation arrangements, payments, notices, service agreements, support obligations and displacement. Then separately analyse whether any legal or rights-based duty applied.

7. ACCESS TO JUSTICE
Record court access, legal aid applications, representation, filing barriers, tribunal processes and review rights. Do not equate an adverse procedural outcome with denial of justice without examining the applicable standard.

8. EFFECTIVE REMEDY
Identify each available remedy: complaint, internal review, tribunal review, judicial review, ombudsman, regulator, human-rights complaint, civil claim, international mechanism. Record whether it was used, exhausted, declined or unavailable and why.

9. INTERNATIONAL MECHANISMS
For ICC, UNHCR and UN special procedures, distinguish legal mandate, admissibility, jurisdiction, registration and substantive finding. A submission is not equivalent to a determination.

10. DIGNITY AND TREATMENT
The archive contains strong language describing alleged treatment. For the analytical record, translate rhetoric into testable events: what was said, by whom, when, in what document, what action followed, and what evidence corroborates it.

11. HUMAN-RIGHTS EVIDENCE MATRIX
RIGHT / PRINCIPLE; FACTUAL EVENT; DECISION-MAKER; LEGAL SOURCE; DOCUMENT; RESPONSE; CONTRARY EVIDENCE; REMEDY; CURRENT STATUS.

12. UNRESOLVED MATTERS
Where the record does not establish the legal threshold, retain the issue as a documented concern or allegation rather than a concluded violation.

13. THEOLOGICAL MATERIAL
The archive’s faith-based interpretation may be preserved in a separate interpretive category. Statements concerning God, divine protection, calling, prophecy or vindication are matters of spiritual testimony and interpretation, not substitutes for documentary or legal findings.

14. CONCLUSION
The human-rights report should demonstrate where the factual record intersects with recognised rights frameworks while leaving legal determination to the competent mechanism.


DOCUMENTARY WARNING
The existence of a document establishes that the document exists. It does not automatically establish the truth of every statement inside it. A complaint establishes that a complaint was made; it does not itself establish the complained-of conduct. A court filing establishes that a filing occurred; it does not establish that the court accepted every proposition. A government record establishes what the government recorded or communicated; it does not necessarily establish that every interpretation of that record is correct. A blockchain timestamp can assist with integrity and chronology; it does not establish the substantive truth of a document.

READER TEST
For every significant proposition ask: Who says this? When did they say it? What did they rely upon? Is the original record available? Was it contemporaneous? Is there independent corroboration? Is there contradictory evidence? Did a competent authority make a finding? What remains unresolved?

EVIDENCE AUDIT CHECKLIST

1. Every major proposition has a unique Evidence ID.
    2. The original document or authoritative source is identified.
    3. The public-facing source link is named and retained.
    4. The document date is distinguished from upload/publication date.
    5. The institution and individual role are recorded precisely.
    6. The evidence classification does not exceed what the source supports.
    7. Allegations are not silently converted into findings.
    8. Later interpretations are not substituted for contemporaneous records.
    9. Independent corroboration is identified separately from repository duplicates.
    10. Contradictory or limiting evidence is disclosed.
    11. Financial figures are reproducible from source records.
    12. Causation is distinguished from temporal sequence.
    13. Institutional knowledge is supported by receipt/acknowledgement evidence.
    14. Legal frameworks are identified without asserting a legal conclusion beyond the source.
    15. Privacy status is recorded before publication.
    16. Repository provenance and version history are preserved.
    17. The reader can return to the primary document without relying on AI summary.
    18. Unresolved questions remain visible rather than being rhetorically closed.

READER INDEPENDENCE STATEMENT

The purpose of this report is to increase the inspectability of the record. A reader should be able to disagree with the archive’s interpretation while still having direct access to the underlying material. The report therefore separates documentary existence, substantive content, corroboration, official findings and interpretation. The final determination of contested facts belongs to the appropriate evidence-review, regulatory, judicial or other competent process.
