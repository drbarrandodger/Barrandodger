AHRC acknowledgment — 4 July 2023

Not a finding that gang-stalking occurred. This file is an Australian Human Rights Commission acknowledgment of a complaint. Receipt and vocabulary on the face of the letter are documentary facts. The merits of the complaint are not decided by this page.

Significance of AHRC using the term “gang stalking”. In much official language the phrase is treated as internet slang or as a symptom label. Here it appears in correspondence from the national human-rights body. That matters for process, not for verdict.

If the Commission accepted and filed a complaint under that heading, the term entered an official docket. It cannot then be dismissed, without more, as a word that no Australian institution will even write down. The legal question the brief asks is whether that filing was then investigated on its merits, or whether the same vocabulary was later used only to pathologise the complainant (see the hospital “delusions / government surveillance” file).

What this letter does not do: it does not find a conspiracy, name a perpetrator, or prove V2K. What it does do: it records that AHRC engaged the complaint in those words. That is the documentary foothold. Read with the raw PDF and the hospital surveillance file.
