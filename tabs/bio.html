Barran Dodger: Bio

Dr Richard William McLean — Barran Dodger

Barran Dodger is the public name under which Dr Richard William McLean publishes his writing, documentary archive and testimony.

Barran Dodger, the public name used by Dr Richard William McLean, is an Australian writer, artist, researcher, documentarian and self-described whistleblower and human-rights advocate.

He describes himself as a writer, artist, researcher, documentarian, whistleblower and human-rights advocate, with a body of work extending across documentary preservation, legal and administrative research, theology, creative writing and public-interest advocacy.

His work brings together documentary evidence, personal testimony, legal and human-rights analysis, creative writing, theology and digital preservation. At the centre of his practice is a sustained effort to preserve and publish the documentary record of his experiences with Australian public institutions and the consequences he says followed from those experiences.

McLean’s work explores institutional accountability, administrative power, disability, social exclusion, homelessness, financial hardship, psychiatric treatment, whistleblowing, legal process, human rights, digital preservation, faith, survival and human dignity.

McLean’s work is also deeply autobiographical. His writing examines experiences of institutional power, disability, psychiatric treatment, social exclusion, homelessness, financial hardship, legal and administrative processes, whistleblowing and the struggle to have his account heard.

Alongside the documentary record is another body of work concerned with faith, suffering, survival, divine providence and the meaning McLean attributes to events in his life. His theological and prophetic writings should therefore be understood as his spiritual interpretation and testimony, rather than as independently established factual or institutional findings.

The name Barran Dodger represents the public identity under which this body of work has developed. It encompasses the author, the archive, the documentary project and the wider creative and theological corpus.
