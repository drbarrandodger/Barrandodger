PID to Sia Lagos — authorised officer Scott Tredwell

Author’s account of the 3 March 2023 Federal Court PID letter. Not a court finding. Name on Court PID procedures is Scott Tredwell, General Counsel (also written Treadwell in later archive commentary).

Primary published text of the letter: Dear Sia Lagos, 3 / 6 March 2023. Federal Court PID assessment file: 27 March 2023 assessment (Backup). Comcare rejection: 26 May 2021 · AAT/ART 12 July 2023.

1. What the PID to Sia Lagos says (main points)

Addressed to Sia Lagos, CEO and Principal Registrar, Federal Court of Australia, copied to Federal Court / FCFCOA PID channels. Date on the face of the letter: 3 March 2023; published heading also uses 6 March 2023. Statutory response window cited: 14 days (to 17 March 2023).

  1. The writer identifies as a current or former public official for PID Act purposes and states he has a DSS employment login.
  2. He names the Court’s authorised PID officers, including Scott Tredwell — General Counsel, and asks that the disclosure be handled under the PID Act.
  3. He describes disclosable conduct in the Act’s own list: corrupt conduct; contravention of law; perverting the course of justice; wastage of public funds; abuse of public trust; unreasonably endangering health and safety; maladministration including conduct that is unjust, oppressive or negligent.
  4. He records that he had already made a related PID to the Commonwealth Ombudsman on 27 February 2023 and treats that as a subsection of the broader Court PID.
  5. He states he is at immediate risk, has no fixed address, and asks for acknowledgement and protection, not merely a file note.

2. Scott Tredwell / Treadwell

On the face of the Court’s own PID procedure as quoted in that letter, Scott Tredwell is an authorised officer to receive and handle PIDs for the Federal Court.

Later archive commentary (author’s account on barrandodger.com) states that Federal Court official Scott Treadwell confirmed DSS employment. That confirmation, if it appears in the 27 March 2023 assessment or a related letter, is a documentary status point — public-official connection for the PID Act — not a finding that every later agency decision was unlawful.

Read the assessment PDF for the operative sentences: 2023-03-27-federal-court-final-assessment-dr-rich-mclean.pdf.

3. Significance of confirming employment status with DSS

If a Federal Court PID officer treats the discloser as a public official connected to DSS, the PID Act pathway is engaged: internal disclosure, authorised-officer duties, confidentiality, and the 14-day action requirement cited in the Lagos letter.

That status sits against a separate compensation pathway that rejected the same person:

  • Comcare determination rejection — 26 May 2021 — PDF
  • Comcare claim 1326583 — PDF
  • AAT/ART McLean and Comcare 2021/7478 — 12 July 2023 — PDF
  • NDIA / WorkSafe evidence rejected FOI letter — PDF

The author’s point is the contradiction: employment/public-official status used (or confirmed) for a Court PID, while WorkCover/Comcare/AAT rejected the compensation claim, and the AAT proceeding ran without legal help. Two systems, same person, opposite practical outcomes. That contrast is an examination question. It is not, by itself, a finding of conspiracy.

4. Federal Court and lesser agencies

The author’s legal structure is that a Federal Court PID assessment sits above Ombudsman non-allocation letters, APRA/IBAC PID rejections, and Comcare/AAT compensation refusals as a higher-court record of how the disclosure was treated. Lesser-agency refusals do not erase the Court file. They also do not mean the Court file “overrules” those agencies as if it were an appeal judgment on Comcare liability. Distinction:

  • Documented on the Court file: PID lodged with the CEO/Registrar; authorised officers named; assessment dated 27 March 2023.
  • Not shown by that fact alone: that Comcare liability is established, or that every named agency committed an offence.

5. Three-point observation from his letter

Taken from the disclosable-conduct list and the protection request in the same 3 March 2023 letter to Lagos:

  1. Maladministration. The letter invokes the PID Act definition: conduct that is unjust, oppressive or negligent. It applies that label to the pattern of referrals, non-responses and closed doors across AFCA, AHRC, Ombudsman and related offices as he experienced them.
  2. Corruption / abuse of public trust / perverting the course of justice. These are the Act’s own categories as copied into the letter. He alleges public officials used process to deny access to AFCA, insurance, equality before the law and a functioning complaint path. Those remain allegations unless a court or authorised investigation establishes them.
  3. Harm / endangerment. The letter says failure to acknowledge the PID “will cause even more harm than has already occurred and may even kill me,” records a prior event described as fatal inside Werribee Mercy, and asks for immediate relief from victimisation.

6. Significance of those three points in a letter that did not protect him

The same document both (a) names the legal duty to protect a discloser and to act within 14 days, and (b) records that he is already homeless, cognitively impaired after what he describes as a fatal event, and without fixed address.

If the Court file later contains an assessment (27 March 2023) but the later archive still shows Ombudsman non-allocation, a 2025 Ombudsman service restriction, Comcare/AAT rejection, and no legal representation at that tribunal, then the author’s observation is: the letter diagnosed maladministration, corruption-category conduct and harm and asked for protection in the same breath, while the subsequent documentary trail is a stack of refusals and restrictions. That is the significance he attaches to Tredwell/Lagos: recognition of process (and, on his account, of DSS employment) without the protection the PID Act text he quoted is supposed to trigger.

Whether that is a failure of duty is a question for the documents and for any competent investigator. This page does not convert the observation into a finding against Sia Lagos or Scott Tredwell personally.
