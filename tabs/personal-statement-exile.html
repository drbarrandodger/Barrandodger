PERSONAL STATEMENT OF EXILE, INSTITUTIONAL DETRIMENT AND THE SEARCH FOR PROTECTION

Author’s original statement, published as supplied 24 September 2026. Not a court finding.

Allegations are allegations unless independently established by a court, tribunal, official investigation or other authoritative finding. Original PDFs: source file 1 · source file 2.

I am carrying what I understand to be the cumulative ethical failures, institutional betrayals and unresolved harms arising across more than three decades of interactions with government agencies, professional systems, health and disability services, legal institutions, oversight bodies and other organisations. I have documented my account and the underlying material in a public archive so that the allegations can be examined against the primary evidence rather than accepted merely because I make them.

The following is my account. Allegations are allegations unless independently established by a court, tribunal, official investigation or other authoritative finding. The archive contains documentary material that I say supports particular aspects of my account, but the existence of a document or correspondence does not by itself establish that the alleged wrongdoing occurred.

MY ALLEGATIONS OF WRONGDOING AND HARM

I allege that I have been subjected to a sustained pattern of institutional and personal harm, including:

  • institutional abandonment and failure of protection;
  • financial restriction and loss of effective access to resources;
  • barriers to legal representation and access to justice;
  • forced dependency upon accommodation and support arrangements from which I say I have had no practical means of escape;
  • administrative and bureaucratic processes that I believe have progressively reduced my ability to advocate for myself;
  • professional and social isolation;
  • characterisation of my complaints as manifestations of mental illness rather than matters requiring independent investigation;
  • what I experience and report as electronic surveillance or unexplained audio harassment, including what I have described as V2K;
  • threats and safety concerns;
  • alleged mistreatment affecting my companion and therapy dog, Crystal;
  • what I allege to be coordinated reputational damage and social exclusion;
  • what I describe as political exile and displacement within the country of my citizenship; and
  • a broader pattern which I allege has operated cumulatively to make it extraordinarily difficult for me to obtain independent legal, financial, medical, housing or media assistance.

THE CENTRAL QUESTION

The central question I ask is:

Can a sequence of individually explainable administrative decisions nevertheless produce a cumulative condition in which a vulnerable person becomes financially dependent, institutionally isolated, deprived of effective legal assistance, psychiatrically discredited and practically unable to escape the circumstances causing the distress?

That is the question I want the evidence to answer.

I do not claim that the answer should be assumed in advance.

I ask that the documentary record be examined to determine whether the cumulative effect described in my account is supported, contradicted or left unresolved by the evidence.

THE FEEDBACK LOOP I ALLEGE

My account describes a potentially self-reinforcing sequence:

financial restriction → dependency → accommodation/support dependency → institutional decision-making → psychiatric interpretation of distress → reduced credibility → reduced access to assistance → greater dependency and distress.

I present this as an analytical model of my allegations, not as an established finding.

The purpose of the model is to explain how apparently separate institutional decisions may interact over time.

It is not intended to establish that every participant consciously coordinated their actions or that every outcome was deliberately caused.

THE EVIDENCE HIERARCHY

Where possible, I want readers to distinguish between different levels of evidentiary status.

  • A — Established fact. A court, tribunal or other authoritative body has made a finding or determination.
  • B — Corroborated claim. The claim is supported by multiple independent records or sources.
  • C — Documented allegation. The allegation is recorded in correspondence, a complaint, statement, transcript or other documentary material.
  • D — Inference. The proposition is an interpretation drawn from documented circumstances rather than a direct finding.
  • E — Speculation. The proposition requires further evidence before it can reasonably be established.

I also distinguish between documentary evidence, corroboration, narrative accounts and analytical interpretation.

My objective is therefore not to ask readers simply to believe me. It is to ask them to test the documentary record.

PURPOSE OF THE ARCHIVE

I publish these materials because I believe that allegations of serious institutional wrongdoing should be capable of independent examination.

I have attempted to preserve the documentary record so that the chronology can be reconstructed and individual allegations can be assessed against the available evidence.

Where the evidence establishes something, it should be identified as established. Where it merely records my allegation, it should be identified as an allegation.

Where the evidence supports a claim but does not independently establish it, it should be identified as corroboration rather than proof.

Where the conclusion is an inference, it should be identified as an inference. Where the evidence remains unresolved, it should remain unresolved.

OPEN CHALLENGE TO THE RECORD

If I am wrong, I ask that the evidence establishing the error be identified.

If my disclosures were properly dealt with, I ask that the record showing how they were received, assessed and answered be identified.

If legal assistance was properly refused or unavailable, I ask that the reasons and applicable review process be identified.

If my account of institutional treatment is mistaken, I ask that the evidence establishing the contrary be identified.

If particular allegations are unsupported, I ask that the evidentiary deficiencies be identified.

If particular allegations are supported, I ask that the supporting evidence be independently recognised and properly examined.

I am not asking an independent reader to assume that every allegation is true.

I am asking that the allegations not be dismissed merely because they are difficult, unconventional, extensive or made by the person who experienced the events.

The appropriate question is what the documentary record demonstrates.

FINAL STATEMENT

My purpose in creating and maintaining this archive is to preserve a record of what I say has happened to me across more than three decades and to make that record available for independent examination.

I allege that the cumulative effect of institutional decisions, financial restrictions, dependency, disability-service involvement, health and mental-health processes, legal barriers, administrative processes, professional responses and social isolation requires examination as a whole rather than solely as a collection of disconnected incidents.

I also preserve my faith-based interpretation of survival, dignity and vindication separately from the documentary claims, because I recognise that matters of faith are different from matters that can be established through documentary or institutional evidence.

The archive is therefore both a record and an invitation.

It is an invitation to journalists, lawyers, researchers, academics, oversight bodies, human-rights organisations, government institutions and independent readers to examine the evidence.

It is an invitation to distinguish established facts from allegations, corroborated claims from inference, and documented material from interpretation.

It is an invitation to examine the chronology rather than relying solely upon institutional summaries or characterisations.

Most importantly, it is an invitation to test the record.

The archive is therefore an invitation to examine the evidence — not a demand that the reader accept my conclusions.
