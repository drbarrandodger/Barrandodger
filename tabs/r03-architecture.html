STANDARD EVIDENCE CLASSIFICATION
A — DOCUMENTED FACT: Supported directly by a reliable primary record or authoritative determination.
B — CORROBORATED CLAIM: Supported by more than one independent or materially corroborating source without necessarily constituting an official finding.
C — DOCUMENTED ALLEGATION: Recorded in a complaint, statement, correspondence or other document but not independently established.
D — INFERENCE: A conclusion drawn from documented circumstances rather than directly established.
E — INTERPRETATION: Analytical, legal, philosophical or theological interpretation.
F — UNRESOLVED: Evidence is incomplete, conflicting, unavailable or insufficient for a stronger classification.

REPORT 3 — THE ARCHITECTURE OF ADMINISTRATIVE ERASURE

PRIMARY FUNCTION
To test whether individually documented administrative decisions, classifications, omissions, referrals, refusals, interventions and institutional responses may have interacted to produce cumulative consequences.

CAUSATION SAFEGUARD
The phrase “architecture of administrative erasure” is retained as the archive’s analytical concept. The report does not assume that an architecture existed as a coordinated scheme. It asks whether the documentary record supports, weakens or leaves unresolved the proposition.

UNIT OF ANALYSIS
INDIVIDUAL DECISION → INSTITUTION → DOCUMENTARY BASIS → IMMEDIATE EFFECT → NEXT INSTITUTIONAL RESPONSE → CUMULATIVE CONSEQUENCE → CONNECTION EVIDENCE → ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATION → CURRENT STATUS.

1. ADMINISTRATIVE CLASSIFICATION
Identify every material classification or status decision. Record who made it, the date, the information considered, the rule or policy cited, the immediate consequence and whether the classification was later changed.

2. INFORMATION FLOW
The central question is not simply whether an institution received a complaint. It is whether the relevant decision-maker actually received the information necessary to understand the issue. A document copied to an institution does not automatically establish that every later decision-maker knew its contents.

3. REFERRAL CHAINS
Map referrals between agencies. For each referral identify: sending institution; receiving institution; date; issue; documents transferred; jurisdiction stated; action requested; response; closure reason; subsequent referral.

4. RECURRING ADMINISTRATIVE MECHANISMS
Test for repeated patterns such as: repeated jurisdictional referrals; repeated requests for more information; repeated closure; repeated reliance on earlier classifications; repeated failure to address a particular question; repeated document discrepancies.

5. MISSING RECORDS
A missing record is not automatically evidence of destruction. Record what is missing, who would ordinarily be expected to hold it, whether retention rules apply, whether a FOI search occurred, and whether any secondary reference confirms that the record once existed.

6. HEALTH / ADMINISTRATION INTERSECTION
Where health-system events intersect with administrative disputes, maintain separate chains for clinical decision-making and administrative decision-making. Do not infer improper motive merely from temporal proximity.

7. EMPLOYMENT / BENEFITS / ADMINISTRATION
Where employment status, compensation, benefits or government administrative classifications overlap, identify each decision separately and link the actual documents.

8. IDENTITY / CORPORATE RECORDS
Where identity or business-registration issues are alleged, compare registration records, filing documents, authorisation evidence, objections and regulator responses. The existence of an unusual registration is not itself proof of fraud.

9. FINANCIAL CONSEQUENCES
Trace only documented causal links. For example: decision → loss of income → documented amount. Do not treat the entire later financial condition as automatically caused by the first decision.

10. HOUSING CONSEQUENCES
Record actual housing events, dates, agreements, notices, payments, support arrangements and displacement. Separate the event from any allegation that an institution intended the consequence.

11. PROFESSIONAL CONSEQUENCES
Separate loss of work, loss of clients, reputation, publication opportunities and alleged professional exclusion. Each requires evidence of the event and, separately, evidence of causation.

12. CUMULATIVE EFFECT
A cumulative effect can be documented without proving a coordinated plan. Multiple independent decisions can have cumulative consequences even where no common actor is established. This is an important alternative hypothesis that must remain visible.

13. COORDINATION TEST
Evidence capable of supporting coordination would include direct communications between actors, common instructions, shared records, explicit cross-references, synchronized actions, admissions or other independent connecting evidence. Temporal sequence alone is weak connecting evidence.

14. ALTERNATIVE EXPLANATIONS
For every proposed connection record plausible alternatives: ordinary administrative process; jurisdictional limitation; resource constraints; independent clinical decision-making; data error; identity confusion; incomplete information; lawful exercise of discretion; unrelated events.

15. KNOWLEDGE THRESHOLD
Replace “everyone knew” with named propositions: Institution A received Document X on Date Y; Officer B acknowledged receipt; the document was attached to Decision C; or the issue was later referred to in Record D.

16. AI ANALYTICAL TEST
AI may identify recurring terms, institutions, dates and document relationships. Such pattern recognition is a lead-generation method. It is not independent proof of coordination.

17. ARCHITECTURE MATRIX
For each alleged component record: Actor; institution; decision; source; date; rule; consequence; next decision; connection evidence; contradiction; alternative explanation; evidentiary level.

18. CONCLUSION TEST
The strongest conclusion available for a chain should never exceed the weakest material link needed to establish the chain. If the link between two events is inferential, the report must say so.


DOCUMENTARY WARNING
The existence of a document establishes that the document exists. It does not automatically establish the truth of every statement inside it. A complaint establishes that a complaint was made; it does not itself establish the complained-of conduct. A court filing establishes that a filing occurred; it does not establish that the court accepted every proposition. A government record establishes what the government recorded or communicated; it does not necessarily establish that every interpretation of that record is correct. A blockchain timestamp can assist with integrity and chronology; it does not establish the substantive truth of a document.

READER TEST
For every significant proposition ask: Who says this? When did they say it? What did they rely upon? Is the original record available? Was it contemporaneous? Is there independent corroboration? Is there contradictory evidence? Did a competent authority make a finding? What remains unresolved?

EVIDENCE AUDIT CHECKLIST

1. Every major proposition has a unique Evidence ID.
    2. The original document or authoritative source is identified.
    3. The public-facing source link is named and retained.
    4. The document date is distinguished from upload/publication date.
    5. The institution and individual role are recorded precisely.
    6. The evidence classification does not exceed what the source supports.
    7. Allegations are not silently converted into findings.
    8. Later interpretations are not substituted for contemporaneous records.
    9. Independent corroboration is identified separately from repository duplicates.
    10. Contradictory or limiting evidence is disclosed.
    11. Financial figures are reproducible from source records.
    12. Causation is distinguished from temporal sequence.
    13. Institutional knowledge is supported by receipt/acknowledgement evidence.
    14. Legal frameworks are identified without asserting a legal conclusion beyond the source.
    15. Privacy status is recorded before publication.
    16. Repository provenance and version history are preserved.
    17. The reader can return to the primary document without relying on AI summary.
    18. Unresolved questions remain visible rather than being rhetorically closed.

READER INDEPENDENCE STATEMENT

The purpose of this report is to increase the inspectability of the record. A reader should be able to disagree with the archive’s interpretation while still having direct access to the underlying material. The report therefore separates documentary existence, substantive content, corroboration, official findings and interpretation. The final determination of contested facts belongs to the appropriate evidence-review, regulatory, judicial or other competent process.
